AI is turning the post‑marketing safety net into a real‑time watchdog. By mining millions of electronic health records (EHRs), machine‑learning algorithms can detect rare adverse events—such as drug‑induced liver injury or cardiotoxicity—that were missed in pre‑marketing trials. [source needed]

Most clinical information is written in free‑text notes, not coded fields. Natural‑language‑processing (NLP) tools (e.g., cTAKES, MedLEE) parse these notes to extract mentions of symptoms and reactions, increasing the sensitivity of pharmacovigilance. [source needed]

When AI aggregates data from multiple sources—clinical trials, post‑marketing reports, and even social‑media chatter—it can generate safety signals within days, rather than months, after an event occurs. [source needed]

Deep‑learning models that ingest a compound’s chemical descriptors and protein‑binding data can forecast off‑target interactions and potential toxicities before the drug enters clinical testing, guiding safer lead optimization. [source needed]

Bayesian networks and other probabilistic models rank detected signals by likelihood and clinical relevance, helping regulators and clinicians focus on the most credible safety concerns and reducing false positives. [source needed]

**Key challenges**

* **Transparency & regulatory acceptance** – Regulators need interpretable models so that safety signals can be trusted and acted upon.  
* **Data harmonization & privacy** – Standardized methods and governance frameworks are required to integrate heterogeneous data (structured EHRs, unstructured notes, social media, genomic data) while protecting patient privacy and ensuring data quality.

Addressing these issues will make AI a trusted partner in keeping medicines safe, providing real‑time vigilance that protects patients, informs clinicians, and guides regulators toward safer medication decisions for all stakeholders. [source needed]
